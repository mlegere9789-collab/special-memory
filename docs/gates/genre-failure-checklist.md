# Genre failure checklist (sanitised abstract lessons, gate G2)

Abstract failure modes that real-time battle games of this kind are known to suffer. They name no product and give no numbers. Each is a test case for AI, simulation and QC.

1. **Blobbing.** Units collapse into a single mass when pathing ignores local density.
2. **Chokepoint stalls.** Large formations jam at gates, bridges and gaps and stop making progress.
3. **Terrain blindness.** Units ignore cover, slope and obstruction when choosing where to stand, move or shoot.
4. **Silly retreats.** Units abandon good positions for no tactical reason or run in circles.
5. **Siege and fortification handling.** Defenders and attackers mishandle walls, gates, entrenchments and fortified lines.
6. **Statistical shortcuts that disagree with played results.** A quick-resolve method that ignores terrain, formation and morale produces outcomes unlike played battles; prefer a method that runs the same rules.
7. **Hidden values that mislead.** The interface shows numbers that do not reflect the values that decide outcomes.
8. **Line-of-sight confusion.** Small terrain features block shots that look clear from the camera, so units shuffle forward to find a firing position.
9. **Unfair difficulty bonuses.** Difficulty is raised by hidden advantages instead of better or worse decisions.
10. **Formation-reshaping stalls.** Units spend long periods re-forming and are helpless while doing so, with no feedback to the player.
11. **Order-latency misreads.** The player cannot tell whether an order is pending, delayed, lost or misinterpreted.
12. **Unreadable morale feedback.** Players cannot tell why a unit is wavering or about to break.
