# CONFLICTS: collisions between SPEC-004 to SPEC-016 and how each was resolved

Eleven specs (006 to 016) were written independently and claimed the same tags, actors, streams, hash positions, tick slots, calibration numbers, amendment labels and files. [REGISTRY.md](REGISTRY.md) holds the resulting values; this file records what collided, where, which resolution was chosen and why. Resolutions change no design except where a registered collision forced a choice, and each such choice is the smallest one that keeps every spec's meaning. Where two designs cannot both stand and the choice is a design decision, the item is in part D and nothing was silently picked.

Counts: 38 collisions resolved (part A), 8 gaps closed (part B), 10 items needing a design decision (part D).

## A. Collisions resolved

**R-01 Payload tag `0x10` (decimal 16), three owners.** SPEC-007 section 9 (Dispatch), SPEC-008 section 8 (scenario hash, "tag 16"), SPEC-011 section 4.1 (Arrive, "tag 16"). Resolution: SPEC-007 keeps `0x10` to `0x13`; SPEC-008 takes `0x20`; SPEC-011 takes `0x30`. Why: SPEC-007 wrote the hex form and a codec with four tags, so it moves least; each spec gets a 16-wide block for growth (REGISTRY 2.2).

**R-02 Payload tag 17 against reserved `0x11`.** SPEC-011 Trigger (17) and SPEC-007 reserved `0x11`. Resolution: SPEC-011 Trigger is `0x31`. Why: same block rule as R-01.

**R-03 Actor `0xFF`, three owners, sequence 0 twice.** SPEC-007 section 9 (scenario script events), SPEC-008 section 8 (tick 0, actor 255, seq 0, scenario hash), SPEC-015 section 3.4 (tick 0, actor `0xFF`, seq 0, difficulty). Resolution: `0xFF` is the system actor (seq 0 scenario binding, seq 1 difficulty, seq 2 and 3 AI configuration); SPEC-007 script events move to `0xFE`. Why: SPEC-002 requires strictly increasing `(tick, actor, seq)`; scripted events can occur at tick 0 with any sequence, so they cannot share an actor with fixed header records.

**R-04 AI seat actor.** SPEC-007 section 9 says `0x80 | side`; SPEC-010 sections 3 and 9 say `AI_ACTOR_BASE 0x40 + side`. Resolution: `0x80`; SPEC-010 uses the SPEC-007 value; the advisor actor `0x60` is unchanged. Why: SPEC-007 owns seats and the direct-order refusal, which must recognise AI seats by actor.

**R-05 Actor 0 against human seat 0.** SPEC-011 section 4.1 and assumption A4 treat actor 0 as free for compiled scripts; SPEC-007 uses `actor = side`, so side 0 is actor 0. Resolution: SPEC-011 compiled inputs use `0xFD`. Script actors and engine-issued orders are never refused by the direct-order rule (REGISTRY 2.1). Why: a history script that shares an actor with a human seat would be refused whenever the command layer is on.

**R-06 Stream base `0x5000_0000`, four owners.** SPEC-006 section 2 (morale, per regiment), SPEC-007 sections 5 and 8 (per message), SPEC-008 section 6.1 (F1 count, per regiment), SPEC-010 section 9 (AI, per side and layer). Resolution: SPEC-006 keeps `0x5000_0000`; messages `0x5100_0000`, F1 count `0x5200_0000`, AI `0x5400_0000`; F1 victim `0x5800_0000` and soldiers `0x4000_0000`, `0x8000_0000` unchanged. Why: stream states are hashed, so a shared base would make an unrelated regiment id equal to a message id and break the independence tests (T6-09, T7-13, T10-06). Message ids are limited to 2^24 so the blocks cannot overlap.

**R-07 Optional hash blocks all placed "after the SPEC-005 blocks"; two without markers.** SPEC-006 section 6 (no marker), SPEC-007 section 9 (`u8 1`), SPEC-009 section 9 (`0x454E5631`). Resolution: fixed order morale, command, environment, each starting with a `u32` magic (`MOR1`, `CMD1`, `ENV1`); `Battle::hash` appends `FID1` (REGISTRY 5). Why: with no marker, "morale on, command off" and "morale off, command on" can write equal bytes; a fixed order makes the stream reproducible whichever layers are on.

**R-08 Five tick orders.** SPEC-005 section 7 (six steps), SPEC-006 section 5 (after step 5, before step 6), SPEC-007 section 9 (`step_command` first), SPEC-008 section 4 (boundary before movement; F1 and F2 steps after hit), SPEC-009 section 9 (periods at ticks 0, 3 mod 4, 19 mod 20). Resolution: one list TK-01 to TK-15 with the pre-step and post-step phases (REGISTRY 6). Why: the specs agree on every pairwise order they state; the single list is their union, so the only choices made were where unrelated layers meet (boundary, then weather, then command, then movement; morale after casualties and before smoke).

**R-09 Fire-phase gates defined in four places.** SPEC-005 section 4 (readiness, `can_fire`, target), SPEC-006 5.8 (`load_gate`, skulkers, morale kinds), SPEC-008 section 4 (shooter class), SPEC-009 section 6 (visibility gate, hold fire). Resolution: seven ordered gates (REGISTRY 6.4). Why: gate order decides draw counts; the registry order keeps the rule that a shot always draws 50 words and a held-fire soldier draws none.

**R-10 `sigma_s` multiplied three ways.** SPEC-005 section 3 (fatigue, smoke factors), SPEC-006 5.8 (`shake_pm`), SPEC-009 section 6 (range-error multiplier, a different quantity). Resolution: SPEC-005 chain, then `shake_pm`, each factor floored separately; the SPEC-009 multiplier applies to the range estimate only (REGISTRY 6.5). Why: integer truncation makes the order observable in hashes.

## D. Needs a design decision

Each item gives the options and a recommendation. Until a decision is recorded (an ADR or a spec amendment citing this id) the ticket named under "Blocks" must not start its affected part; everything else in the roadmap proceeds.

**D-01 Smoke: sector tiles or one grid.** SPEC-008 section 7 gives each Hot sector its own `SmokeGrid` tile, zeroed on entry, freed on exit only when its smoke total is 0, hashed ascending. SPEC-009 section 4 updates one map-wide grid every fourth tick with exact mass conservation, flow to neighbours and loss only at the map edge. Smoke that diffuses or drifts out of a Hot sector into a Cold one has no tile to land in, so the two cannot both be exact. Options: (a) the whole-map grid is authoritative and tiles are a lazy memory layout with no effect on results; (b) tiles are authoritative, SPEC-009's update runs per tile with explicit neighbour flow and a Hot set that grows to every sector holding or receiving smoke; (c) drop SPEC-008's smoke tiles. Recommendation: (a). A 10 km map at 8 m cells is about 1.56 million cells, 6.2 MB, far inside SPEC-008's memory budget; SPEC-009's bounding-rectangle trimming already makes the cost follow the active area; and it removes edge-of-sector artefacts. Blocks: the smoke-tile part of T-087 (and SPEC-005 A15), and the smoke content of the FID1 block.

**D-02 One elevation source, flat or ground-relative ballistics.** SPEC-009 assumption A3 and SPEC-005 keep projectiles on a flat datum (`z` is height above local ground; relief changes sight only). SPEC-014 section 14 requires ground contact at `z <= height_mm(x, y)`, obstacles spanning `ground + height`, muzzle at `ground + posture` and ground-relative hit heights (SPEC-005 A16). There are also three elevation structures (SPEC-009 `ElevationGrid` 8 m in decimetres, SPEC-012 `Relief`, SPEC-014 `TerrainWorld` 4 m in centimetres) and three line-of-sight rules with different steps and rounding, while T10-28 needs the AI's `line_clear` to agree with what the fire phase allows. Options: (a) flat ballistics, relief for sight only; one `ElevationGrid` produced from the pack by a new `hd-terrain export-relief --cell-mm 8000`; `Relief` and `line_clear` are built from it; `terrain::los` stays a cross-check within CT-014-1; A16 is dropped; (b) ground-relative ballistics as in SPEC-014 (changes every F0 golden and adds a height lookup per sub-step); (c) terrain-following: flat integration, ground contact and hit heights measured against the ground at the point. Recommendation: (a) for the slab and the first playable milestone; revisit only if playtest shows shots passing through ridges, which the visibility gate already prevents. Blocks: T-094 (source of heights; hand-made grids may be used meanwhile), T-105 (AI line of sight), the SPEC-005 A16 amendment, T-124 beyond scene-local tables.

**D-03 Two observation systems.** SPEC-007 section 7: every 100 ticks each regiment observes enemy regiments with a distance, cover and smoke quality formula and three random draws, feeding pictures; contacts never carry a `RegimentId`. SPEC-009 section 8: observers (regiments, headquarters, stations) every 20 ticks use ray-tested `sight`, no draws, writing a `SightLog` keyed by the true `RegimentId`, "the only thing headquarters may read". They overlap and disagree on the quality formula and on who may hold true ids. Options: (a) layered: SPEC-009 supplies the visibility oracle (visible, transmit, range) and SPEC-007 consumes it when `env` is `Some`, adds its noise and strips ids; with `env` `None` its own formula stands; (b) SPEC-007 only, SPEC-009 keeps `sight` and `signal_quality` and drops observers; (c) both live, pictures from SPEC-007, `SightLog` for audit. Recommendation: (a), the only option in which smoke and relief change what commanders know. T-075 should read visibility through a small trait so (a) and (b) both fit. Blocks: T-075 final form and T-096; decide before T-076.

**D-04 Morale report shape.** SPEC-006 section 5.7 exposes `MoraleReport { indicator (6 values), up to two causes of at least cause_min, fire_pm, shake_pm, looking, absent, thinning_pm }` and never `c` or `loss`. SPEC-015 sections 3 and 6 need `level` (5 values), `margin` above the next worse level, `trend`, and a factor list that sums to the model total (T15-06 asserts top three and the sum). SPEC-006 supplies no margin, trend or total. Options: (a) extend the SPEC-006 report with a quantised margin band (steps of 50 permille), `trend`, and all nine cause terms in permille of the ceiling, still never raw `c` or `loss`; (b) cut SPEC-015 to the SPEC-006 shape (level from the indicator, two causes, no margin or trend; T15-06 rewritten); (c) let the interface compute margin from public numbers (breaks "no statistic of its own", T15-02). Recommendation: (a); SPEC-006 already ranks nine causes internally and the quantised margin gives the early warning SPEC-015 wants without exposing the hidden value. Blocks: T-064 (report), T-152, T-159.

**D-05 Order status machine and timing.** SPEC-007 section 8 derives `Pending`, `InTransit`, `Overdue`, `Lost`, `Delivered`, `Misread`, `Modified`, `Declined`, `Stale` with `ack_deadline = eta_hi` plus the return trip and `lost_grace_ticks 2400`; T7-12 pins `Overdue` to `(ack_deadline, ack_deadline + grace]`. SPEC-015 section 5 derives `Drafted`, `InTransit`, `Delayed`, `Overdue`, `PresumedLost`, `Received`, `Executing`, `Completed`, `Misread`, `Lost` with `grace = (eta_hi - sent) * 500 / 1000` and no `Declined`, `Modified` or `Stale`; T15-05 pins different boundaries. Options: (a) SPEC-007 owns states and timings; SPEC-015 renders `OrderView.status`, adds `Drafted` locally, derives `Delayed` from `eta_hi0`, drops `presume_lost_pm`, and T15-05 follows the SPEC-007 boundaries; (b) SPEC-015's rules replace SPEC-007's (T7-12 rewritten, three states lost from the information model); (c) keep both (two truths for one checklist item). Recommendation: (a). Blocks: T-074 (status views), T-152, T-159.

**D-06 Scripted history: scenario rows or replay script.** SPEC-008 carries arrivals, departures, triggers and weather in the scenario and applies them in `Battle::step`; the replay holds only the scenario hash. SPEC-011 compiles `script.toml` (arrivals, orders, triggers) to replay inputs applied by a `ReplayDriver` that wraps a `BattleState`, reads units from `units.toml`, and has its own `history_hash`. Two files would hold the same historical times, and the driver cannot see class hand-overs. Options: (a) one owner per row: arrivals, departures and weather stay in the SPEC-008 scenario; SPEC-011 scripts keep `[[order]]`, `[[observe]]`, trigger hooks and `[[override]]` rows (early and late variants replace `at_s` of a scenario row); the driver wraps `Battle` and the `Arrive` payload is dropped; (b) SPEC-011 owns everything historical and scenarios carry only the order of battle; (c) disjoint scopes: SPEC-008 rows for synthetic and auto-resolve scenarios, SPEC-011 scripts for calibration vignettes. Recommendation: (a), a single source for each historical time with the evidence window already in the scenario row. Blocks: T-111 (schema), T-112 (driver), T-114 (data); decide before T-111.

**D-07 Aggregate casualties are invisible.** SPEC-008 section 6.2 changes aggregate counts without writing `Casualty` records; SPEC-006 section 1 claims morale runs identically in every class and reads the log; SPEC-011 counts log records and assumes day-level vignettes (F1 or F2) expose the same log. An F2 regiment would never lose morale and F2 vignettes would show no casualties. Options: (a) F2 outcomes, and F1 hits on aggregates, append `Casualty` records with a sentinel `target_index` (`u32::MAX`); consumers skip the agent-specific checks for it; (b) a separate aggregate log consumed by SPEC-006 and SPEC-011; (c) morale off for F2 and SPEC-011 counts from aggregate counters (amending SPEC-006 section 1). Recommendation: (a), the smallest change for consumers and hash-stable. Blocks: T-086, T-063 (ingestion), T-113 (metrics); decide before T-063.

**D-08 Difficulty has two definitions.** SPEC-010 section 4 defines AI levels `easy`, `standard`, `hard` as behaviour rows and forbids rows that name sight range or message delay. SPEC-015 section 10 defines `Difficulty { ai_initiative_pm, ai_risk_pm, info_noise_pm, courier_spread_pm, assists }`; the last keys scale sighting error and courier spread (SPEC-007 and SPEC-009 values) and have no counterpart in the AI rows; the replay records differ (tag `0x40` and `0xA1`). Options: (a) the player option selects a SPEC-010 level per side; `ai_initiative_pm` and `ai_risk_pm` become the preset's mapping onto the three rows, `info_noise_pm` and `courier_spread_pm` become symmetric scenario handicaps applied identically to both sides' information model (two scenario fields in SPEC-007), `assists` stay interface only; (b) extend the SPEC-010 rows with the SPEC-015 keys; (c) apply the SPEC-015 keys to the human only (rejected: breaks genre checklist item 9). Recommendation: (a). Blocks: T-156, T-108.

**D-09 Commander traits applied twice.** SPEC-007 uses traits for staff delay (`process_pm`) and for `interpret` (go score, reach, decline) on receipt; SPEC-010 uses them for utility weights and a discretion gate before issuing. With SPEC-010's fallback (`Move` plus `Hold`), one discretionary objective can be declined by the AI gate and then again by `interpret`, and the delay counts as well. Options: (a) accept both, document that they model different decisions, and have the AI send `Strict` once its own gate approved; (b) remove the AI gate in favour of `interpret`; (c) skip `interpret` for AI-issued orders. Recommendation: (a). Blocks: T-107 only.

**D-10 Host audio model: prototype baseline or production.** SPEC-016 section 7.2 and `perf-kit` aggregate by `(regiment, 50 m cell)` in centimetres, use a fixed 343 m/s delay in samples, and select 48 clusters and 64 voices; SPEC-012 aggregates by `(source, unit, 100 m cell)`, derives delay from temperature and wind, budgets 32 sources plus a bed, and models shadow by gradients. Options: (a) `perf-kit` is a prototype baseline for the M1 measurements only; the production host consumes SPEC-012 `MixPacket`; the M1 ADR records the choice and H16-9's "delay error 0 samples" is restated against SPEC-012's `delay_ms`; (b) rebuild `perf-kit` on SPEC-012 now; (c) SPEC-012 adopts the SPEC-016 aggregation. Recommendation: (a); the prototype exists to measure frame cost, not to fix the acoustic model. Blocks: nothing before the M1 gate (ADR 0009).
